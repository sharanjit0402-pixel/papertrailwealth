## 1. The markets: a seven-day losing streak

It was a week most investors would like to forget.

The **S&P 500 fell 8.61%** to 6,910.39. That's its worst week since April 2025 and its **seventh losing day in a row**, the longest streak since February 2020. The **Nasdaq Composite fell 10%**, its biggest weekly drop since March 2020, and it's now **negative for 2026**. The **Dow dropped 8%**, also its worst week since March 2020.

Friday did the most damage. On November 20, the S&P 500 lost 3.7%, the Nasdaq lost 5.3% and the Dow lost 1.9% in a single session.

**What "correction" means:** A correction is when an index falls 10% or more from its most recent high. The S&P 500 is now about **11.6% below its October 6 record** of 7,818.93, and the Dow is about 10% below its peak. If the drop reaches 20%, it becomes a **bear market**.

**Where the pain was worst:** Chip stocks. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost 10.7% on Friday alone, and chipmakers lost a reported **$2.3 trillion** in market value over the week. Nvidia, Micron, Marvell, CoreWeave and Nebius each fell more than 20%.

**What held up better:** Smaller and "boring" stocks fell less. The Dow, full of older blue-chip companies, dropped less than the tech-heavy Nasdaq on Friday.

## 2. The big story: Nvidia

Nvidia is the company at the center of the AI boom, so when it stumbles, the whole market feels it.

- **The good news:** On Tuesday, Nvidia reported third-quarter revenue that **more than doubled** from a year earlier, with a gross profit margin near 75%, and it raised its outlook. In a normal week, that would send a stock soaring.
- **The bad news:** It was Nvidia's first report since co-founder **Jensen Huang resigned as CEO** earlier this month. CFO Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO, and the board said it does not plan to bring Huang back. Then on Friday, a **report of an early-stage SEC accounting inquiry** sent the stock down another 14%.

Nvidia closed the week at **$125.53, down 22.9%**, its worst week since 2008. The stock is now down nearly **50% from its October record** of $239.24.

**Why it matters for regular investors:** Nvidia is one of the largest companies in the world, so it makes up a big slice of popular index funds. If you own an S&P 500 or Nasdaq fund in your 401(k), Nvidia's slide is part of why your balance dropped this week.

## 3. Anthropic's IPO: a record debut in a rough week

**Anthropic**, the AI company behind the Claude assistant, went public on the Nasdaq on Wednesday, November 19, in the biggest IPO on record. Shares were priced at **$83**, first traded at **$111.50** and closed the day at **$97.17**, up about 17% from the IPO price.

But the excitement didn't last. Anthropic shares slid on Friday along with the rest of the AI sector. It's a good reminder of how IPOs work: the people who bought at the opening trade paid far more than the IPO price, and many of them were already sitting on losses by the end of day one.

## 4. The Fed and interest rates

The Federal Reserve meets on **December 8–9**, and markets now expect it to **raise** interest rates, not cut them.

Here's why. October's inflation report, released on November 10, came in hotter than expected:

- Overall prices (CPI) rose **0.4%** in October.
- "Core" prices, which leave out food and energy, rose **0.3%** for the **third month in a row**.

Fed Governor **Christopher Waller** said that if core inflation came in at 0.3% or higher, a **half-point (0.50%) hike** should be considered in December. It did. Futures markets now see a December hike as very likely, with a smaller chance of the larger half-point move. The Fed's key rate currently sits at **3.75%–4.00%**.

**What a rate hike means for you:**

- **Credit cards and car loans** usually get more expensive soon after.
- **Savings accounts and CDs** usually pay more.
- **Stocks** often struggle when rates rise, because borrowing costs more and safer investments start paying better.

## 5. Bonds, gold, oil and Bitcoin

- **Treasury bonds:** When stocks fell, investors bought bonds for safety. The **10-year Treasury yield fell about 0.20 points to roughly 4.95%**, back under 5% after spending much of the year above it. When bond prices go up, yields go down.
- **The U.S. dollar** stayed near its **highest level in about 19 months**.
- **Gold** fell, not rose, dropping to its lowest level since late summer 2025. A strong dollar and expectations of higher rates both weigh on gold.
- **Oil** fell for a third straight week, with U.S. crude in the low $80s, on strong supply and worries about slower growth.
- **Bitcoin** fell for a **seventh straight week**, trading around $60,000, down from roughly $86,000 in early October.

## 6. Washington: what changed this week

**A new Congress is coming.** In the November 3 midterm elections, **Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate**, starting in January. Some analysts say a less friendly legislative climate for big tech companies is one more worry weighing on tech stocks.

**The "lame-duck" session.** Until January, the current Republican-led Congress is still in charge. Its top job is keeping the government funded. **Government funding runs out on December 11**, so watch for a deal (or a shutdown fight) when Congress returns from its Thanksgiving break.

**A stock-trading ban for Congress?** On November 17, a group of current and incoming House Democrats pledged to push for a ban on members of Congress trading individual stocks, and Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries said it will be an early priority in the new Congress. The House passed a narrower version in July, but it stalled in the Senate in September.

**Tariff refunds keep flowing.** After the Supreme Court ruled in February that a 1977 emergency law (IEEPA) doesn't let the president impose tariffs, U.S. Customs has been processing refunds to businesses. A recent update put potential refunds at roughly **$138 billion**, money that flows back to companies that paid those tariffs.

## 8. Money term of the week: the VIX

The **VIX** is often called Wall Street's **"fear index."** It measures how much big price swings investors expect in the S&P 500 over the next 30 days.

- A VIX **under 20** usually means calm markets.
- A VIX **above 30** means investors are nervous and expect big swings.

This week, the VIX jumped about **86%** and closed Friday at **35.89**, one of its highest levels of the year. A high VIX doesn't tell you which way stocks will go next. It just tells you that investors expect a bumpy ride.

## The bottom line

This was a painful week, led by the AI stocks that drove so much of the market's gains. But corrections are a normal part of investing. The S&P 500 has dropped 10% or more at some point in roughly half of all years since 1980, and it has still grown over time.

If you invest for retirement through a 401(k) or IRA, a week like this is mostly a test of patience. The money you add during a downturn buys shares at lower prices.
