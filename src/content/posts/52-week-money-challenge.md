What if you could save **$1,378** this year, starting with a single dollar?

That's the 52-Week Money Challenge, one of the most popular saving challenges in America. It works because it starts so small that it feels almost silly, and by the time the amounts get bigger, saving has become a habit.

## How it works

- **Week 1:** save $1
- **Week 2:** save $2
- **Week 3:** save $3
- ...and so on, adding one dollar each week
- **Week 52:** save $52

Add it all up and you have **$1,378** by the end of the year.

## How fast it grows

| By the end of week | You've saved |
|---|---|
| 10 | $55 |
| 20 | $210 |
| 26 (halfway) | $351 |
| 30 | $465 |
| 40 | $820 |
| 52 | **$1,378** |

Notice something? Halfway through the year, you've only saved about a quarter of the total. The second half does the heavy lifting. That's exactly why so many people quit around week 30: the deposits feel bigger, right when the holidays hit.

## 3 versions that are easier to finish

### 1. The reverse challenge

Start with **$52 in week 1** and go *down* by a dollar each week. You save the same $1,378, but the biggest deposits come in January, when motivation is highest, and the smallest land in November and December, when money is tight.

### 2. The "pick any square" challenge

Write the numbers 1 to 52 on a sheet. Each week, save any amount from the list and cross it off. Tight week? Pick $3. Payday week? Knock out $50. Same total, total flexibility.

### 3. The half challenge

Save 50 cents more each week instead of a dollar. Week 1 is $0.50, week 52 is $26. You'll end the year with **$689**. That's still a real emergency cushion.

## Tips to actually finish

1. **Automate it.** Set up a weekly automatic transfer to a separate savings account. Most banks let you schedule it in a couple of minutes.
2. **Keep it separate.** Money in your checking account gets spent. A separate account, ideally one that pays interest, keeps it out of sight.
3. **Track it visually.** A simple paper tracker on the fridge, where you color in each week, is surprisingly motivating.
4. **Missed a week? Don't quit.** Just double up next week, or switch to the "pick any square" version.

## What to do with the $1,378

Good uses for your challenge money:

- Start an emergency fund. (Read: [How to Build an Emergency Fund From $0](/blog/emergency-fund-from-zero/).)
- Pay down a high-interest credit card.
- Pay for next year's holidays in cash instead of on a card.

**The one-line lesson:** Big savings rarely start with a big number. They start with a dollar and a habit.
