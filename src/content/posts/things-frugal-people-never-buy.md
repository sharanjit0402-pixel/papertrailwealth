Frugal people aren't cheap. They just refuse to pay for things that don't make their life better.

Here are 25 things they skip, and what they do instead. You don't need to cut all of them. Pick five, and you could free up **$100 to $300 a month.**

## Around the house

**1. Paper towels by the dozen.** They use washable cloths and keep one roll for emergencies.

**2. Name-brand cleaning sprays.** Vinegar, water and a little dish soap clean most surfaces for pennies.

**3. Bottled water.** A filter pitcher or tap filter costs less than a few weeks of bottles.

**4. Dryer sheets.** Wool dryer balls last for years.

**5. Extended warranties on small electronics.** Many credit cards already add extra warranty coverage, and most gadgets don't break in the extra period.

## Food and drinks

**6. Daily coffee shop drinks.** A $6 latte five days a week is about **$1,500 a year**. They brew at home and keep the café for a treat.

**7. Pre-cut fruit and vegetables.** You pay double for someone else's knife work.

**8. Name-brand basics.** Store brands of flour, sugar, spices, cereal and medicine are often made to the same standard for much less.

**9. Lunch out every workday.** Packing lunch three days a week can save over $1,000 a year.

**10. Food that goes to waste.** They plan meals, shop with a list, and eat what's in the fridge before buying more.

**11. Groceries on an empty stomach.** Hungry shopping means impulse buying. They eat first.

## Subscriptions and bills

**12. Subscriptions they forgot about.** They check their bank statement every month and cancel anything unused.

**13. Every streaming service at once.** They rotate: subscribe to one, watch what they want, cancel, switch.

**14. The phone company's premium plan.** Smaller carriers often use the same networks for a fraction of the price.

**15. Gym memberships they don't use.** Walking, running and free workout videos cost nothing.

**16. Bank accounts with monthly fees.** Plenty of banks and credit unions offer free checking.

## Shopping habits

**17. Things just because they're on sale.** A 50% discount on something you didn't need is still 100% wasted.

**18. Fast fashion.** They buy fewer, better clothes that last for years.

**19. Brand-new cars.** A new car can lose a big chunk of its value in the first few years. They buy reliable used cars and keep them a long time.

**20. "Buy now, pay later" for small stuff.** If they can't pay for it today, they wait.

**21. Single-use party supplies.** Reusable plates and decorations get used again and again.

**22. Books and movies they'll only use once.** The public library is free and often has e-books and audiobooks too.

## Money moves

**23. Lottery tickets.** The odds of winning a big jackpot are worse than one in hundreds of millions. They invest that money instead.

**24. Interest on credit cards.** They pay the full balance every month, so the card company never earns a cent from them.

**25. A savings account earning almost nothing.** They keep their emergency fund in a high-yield savings account and invest money for the long term.

## What the swaps add up to

Here's what just four common cuts could save:

| Swap | Yearly savings (about) |
|---|---|
| Home coffee instead of $6 lattes, 5x a week | $1,300 |
| Packing lunch 3 days a week | $1,000 |
| Canceling 2 unused subscriptions | $300 |
| Store brands on basics | $400 |
| **Total** | **about $3,000 a year** |

Invest that **$250 a month** at a 7% average return, and in 20 years it could grow to about **$130,000.** Same lifestyle, just fewer leaks. ([Here's how to start investing with as little as $100.](/blog/how-to-start-investing-with-100-dollars/))

## The bottom line

Frugal living isn't about saying no to everything. It's about saying no to the things you won't remember, so you can say yes to the things you will.

**The one-line lesson:** Every dollar you don't waste is a dollar that can start working for you.
