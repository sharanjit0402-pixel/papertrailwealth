Most people think investing starts at $10,000. It doesn't. Today, **you can start with $100, or even $1.**

Here's a simple plan for your first $100, and what happens if you keep going.

> **Quick note:** This article is general education, not investment advice. Investments can lose value, and returns are never guaranteed.

## First: is your $100 ready to invest?

Before you invest, check three things:

1. **No high-interest credit card debt.** Cards often charge over 20% a year. Paying that off is a guaranteed "return" no investment can promise.
2. **A small emergency cushion.** Even $500 to $1,000 in savings keeps you from selling investments when your car breaks down. (Our [Emergency Fund Challenge](/challenges/emergency-fund/) helps you build one.)
3. **You won't need this money for at least 5 years.** Markets can drop in the short term. Time smooths it out.

All three? Your $100 is ready.

## The 4-step plan for your first $100

### Step 1: Open a free brokerage account

Big brokers like Fidelity, Charles Schwab and Vanguard charge **no account fees** and **no trading commissions** on stocks and ETFs. It takes about 15 minutes online.

### Step 2: Choose a Roth IRA or a regular account

- **Roth IRA:** For retirement money. You invest money you've already paid tax on, and the growth can come out **tax-free** in retirement. A great first account for most young earners.
- **Regular brokerage account:** For goals before retirement. More flexible, but growth is taxed.

### Step 3: Buy one broad index fund

Don't try to pick the next hot stock with your first $100. Buy **one S&P 500 or total stock market index fund**. It spreads your money across hundreds of companies at once, with a tiny yearly fee. ([New to index funds? Start here.](/blog/index-funds-for-beginners/))

Can't afford a full share? Most big brokers let you buy **fractional shares**, so $100 buys exactly $100 worth.

### Step 4: Set up automatic monthly investing

This is the step that actually makes people rich. Set your account to invest a fixed amount, like **$100 on the 1st of every month**, automatically.

You'll never have to remember, decide, or worry about timing.

## What $100 a month can grow into

Assuming a **7% average yearly return** (a long-term example, not a promise):

| Years | You put in | Could grow to |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | $12,000 | **about $17,300** |
| 20 | $24,000 | **about $52,100** |
| 30 | $36,000 | **about $122,000** |
| 40 | $48,000 | **about $262,500** |

Look at the last row. You put in **$48,000**, and the market adds over **$214,000** on top. That's compound growth: your money earns money, and then that money earns money too.

## Where to find $100 a month

If $100 sounds like a lot, it's usually hiding in small leaks:

- One streaming service you forgot about: about $15
- Two takeout meals a month: about $40
- Bank fees or a gym you don't use: about $45

Find three leaks, and your investing money pays for itself. (See [25 Things Frugal People Never Buy](/blog/things-frugal-people-never-buy/) for more ideas.)

## Mistakes to avoid with your first $100

- **Checking it every day.** It will go up and down. That's normal. Check once a month at most.
- **Chasing hype.** Trending stocks and coins can crash fast. Boring index funds win long term.
- **Stopping when the market drops.** Drops mean you're buying at a discount. Keep your automatic investing on.

## The bottom line

The amount matters less than the habit. $100 a month, started today, beats $1,000 a month "someday."

**The one-line lesson:** You don't need a lot of money to start investing. You need to start investing to have a lot of money.
