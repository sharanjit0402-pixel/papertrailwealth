Federal Reserve surveys have found that roughly **1 in 3 American adults couldn't cover a surprise $400 expense with cash**. A car repair, a vet bill or a broken phone, and suddenly it goes on a credit card that charges 20% interest or more.

An emergency fund is the fix. It's simply money set aside for the unexpected, so a bad week doesn't turn into months of debt. Here's how to build one, even if you're starting at zero.

## Step 1: Set a starter goal of $500 to $1,000

Forget "six months of expenses" for now. That number is so big it stops most people before they start.

Your first goal is a **starter fund of $500 to $1,000**. That covers most everyday emergencies: a new tire, an urgent-care visit, a broken appliance.

## Step 2: Open a separate savings account

Keep your emergency money **away from your checking account**. If it sits next to your spending money, it gets spent.

Look for an account that is:

- **Separate** from your everyday bank account, so it's out of sight
- **Easy to reach** in a day or two when a real emergency hits
- **Insured.** In the US, bank deposits are generally protected by the FDIC up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category.
- **Paying interest.** Many online savings accounts pay far more interest than a standard checking account.

## Step 3: Find your first $100 fast

Quick wins build momentum:

- **Sell one thing** you don't use: old electronics, furniture, clothes.
- **Cancel one subscription** you forgot about and move that money over.
- **Do a no-spend weekend.** Cook at home and skip shopping for two days.
- **Save any "found" money:** a refund, a rebate or a birthday check.

## Step 4: Automate it

Set up an **automatic transfer on payday**, even if it's just $20 or $25. Money you never see is money you don't miss.

| If you save every week | You'll have in 6 months | In 1 year |
|---|---|---|
| $10 | $260 | $520 |
| $20 | $520 | $1,040 |
| $40 | $1,040 | $2,080 |

Small amounts add up faster than you think. Want a fun way to do it? Try [the 52-Week Money Challenge](/blog/52-week-money-challenge/).

## Step 5: Decide what counts as an emergency

Write it down so you're not tempted later. A good rule: **it's an emergency if it's unexpected, necessary and urgent.**

- **Emergency:** car repair to get to work, a medical bill, a sudden job loss, an essential appliance breaking.
- **Not an emergency:** a sale, concert tickets, a holiday you knew was coming. (Those deserve their own savings goals.)

## Step 6: Grow it to 3 to 6 months

Once your starter fund is in place, and especially after high-interest debt is under control, keep going toward roughly **3 to 6 months of essential expenses**: rent, food, utilities, insurance and minimum payments.

If your income is irregular, or you're the only earner in your home, aiming for the higher end gives you more breathing room.

## Step 7: Refill it after you use it

Using your emergency fund isn't failure. It's the whole point. When you dip into it, pause extra goals for a bit and **refill it first**.

**The one-line lesson:** An emergency fund doesn't make emergencies disappear. It makes them just another bill instead of a crisis.
