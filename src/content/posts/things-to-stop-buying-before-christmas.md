December is the most expensive month of the year for most families. But here's the part nobody tells you: **a lot of what you buy in December gets cheaper on December 26.**

Stores order huge amounts of holiday stock. Whatever doesn't sell by Christmas becomes a problem they want gone, fast. That's your chance.

Here are 8 things to stop buying before Christmas, and when to buy them instead.

## 1. Wrapping paper, bows and gift bags

Holiday wrapping supplies stop selling the moment the presents are opened, so stores mark them down hard, usually starting **December 26**. The longer you wait, the deeper the discount, but the smaller the selection.

**Do this instead:** Buy only what you need for this year. Then stock up after Christmas on plain or solid-color paper and gift bags you can use for birthdays all year.

## 2. Holiday decorations

Lights, ornaments, wreaths and even artificial trees go on clearance right after the holiday. Discounts often reach well past half off by early January.

**Do this instead:** Decorate with what you have this year. Make a short list of what you actually want, and buy it on clearance for next Christmas.

## 3. Christmas cards

Boxed cards are a classic after-Christmas markdown.

**Do this instead:** Send digital cards or a photo text this year, and buy next year's boxed cards in the clearance aisle.

## 4. Winter coats and boots

This surprises people. Coats feel like a December purchase, but **January is usually the cheaper month.** Stores clear winter clothing to make room for spring styles, so coats, boots, gloves and sweaters often drop further than they did on Black Friday.

**Do this instead:** If your family can get through a few more weeks with what you have, wait for the January clearance racks.

## 5. Bedding and towels

January is famous for **"white sales,"** when stores discount sheets, comforters, towels and other linens.

**Do this instead:** Put "new sheets" on your January list, not your December one.

## 6. Rush shipping

Paying $10 to $25 extra to get a gift by Christmas Eve is one of the easiest ways to waste money in December.

**Do this instead:** Order early, choose free standard shipping, or use free in-store pickup. A late gift with a kind note beats an expensive express fee.

## 7. Checkout-lane "stocking stuffers"

Those little bins of candy, gadgets and novelty socks near the register are designed for impulse buys. A few dollars here and there quietly adds up to a lot.

**Do this instead:** Plan stocking stuffers ahead with a set budget, and walk past the bins.

## 8. Anything on a brand-new store credit card

"Save 20% today when you open a card!" sounds great. But many store cards charge very high interest. If you carry a balance into the new year, the interest can easily wipe out the discount.

**Do this instead:** Only take the deal if you will pay the full balance on the first bill. Otherwise, skip it.

## What you *should* still buy before Christmas

Waiting doesn't work for everything. Buy these now:

- **The actual gifts** people are expecting on Christmas morning
- **Food for your holiday meals**, which doesn't go on sale after the day it's needed
- **Popular toys**, which can sell out completely before the 25th
- **Anything you need for travel**, since last-minute prices tend to rise

## The simple holiday rule

Before you buy something in December, ask one question: **"Do I need this before the 25th?"**

If the answer is no, it will very likely be cheaper in a few weeks.

**The one-line lesson:** The best Christmas deals start the day after Christmas.
